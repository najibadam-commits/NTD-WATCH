# Stage 6 stakeholder session pack

**Status:** session materials, prepared 2026-10-18. **No session has been held. No participant has been contacted. This pack must not be used until the ethics and participant determination for the validation activities is recorded (ETH-1 in `PRIVACY_ETHICS_VALIDATION_WORKPLAN.md`) and the sponsor has ratified the decision criteria (S6-02).** Nothing here is a finding. This pack does not conduct or simulate stakeholder research.

Companion documents: `STAKEHOLDER_VALIDATION_PLAN.md` (questions and assumptions), `STAGE6_EVIDENCE_REGISTER.md` (where coded findings go), `STAGE6_DECISION_REGISTER.md` section 3 (locked criteria), `DIFFERENTIATION_VALIDATION.md` (hypotheses).

## 1. Purpose

To learn, from the people who do the work, **what happens today after a potential NTD-related signal** and whether a lightweight, interoperable community-to-action workflow could close a real operational gap, or whether the gap does not exist, is already closed, or is better addressed inside an existing platform. The session is a conversation about current practice. It is not a demonstration, a sales conversation or a test of the participant.

A session succeeds when we learn something reliable. "This is not a problem here" and "this is already handled by X" are successful results.

## 3. Neutral introduction (read or adapt; do not embellish)

> Thank you for your time. I am [role, organisation]. We are trying to understand how things work today after a community worker notices someone who might need attention for a neglected tropical disease, for example a skin change or swelling. We would like to learn from you what happens, what works, and what does not.
>
> There are no right or wrong answers, and we are not assessing you or your work. We are not selling anything. A digital idea is being considered, and one possible result of this study is that it is not needed, or that it should be changed, or that it should be built into a system that already exists. Any of these is a useful answer.
>
> You can skip any question and stop at any time without any effect on your work. We will not ask you to share names or details that identify any patient or community member. [State how notes are recorded and stored, as approved.] May I continue?

Do **not** describe NTD-WATCH, its benefits, or call it a solution. Do not say the prototype "helps". If asked what the project is, give the neutral description above and, if the ethics approval allows, the one-sentence description from `docs/STAKEHOLDER_REVIEW.md` section 1.

## 5. Session structure (30 to 45 minutes)

| Time (minutes) | Part                             | Content                                                                                                             |
| -------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0 to 5         | Opening                          | Neutral introduction, consent, role and setting questions                                                           |
| 5 to 25        | Current practice (core)          | Questions 1 to 8 as relevant to the participant type, open questions first, in the order of the pathway             |
| 25 to 33       | Information and decisions        | Burden today; decisions that depend on information; systems in use (Q8 to Q11 as relevant)                          |
| 33 to 40       | Neutral description and reaction | A neutral description of the proposed steps (section 6.3) **only if** the first parts are complete; then Q9 and Q12 |
| 40 to 45       | Close                            | Disconfirmation questions; who else to ask; thanks; what happens to the notes                                       |

Rules: ask about current practice before any description of the proposal. If time runs short, cut the neutral description, not the current-practice questions. The prototype is **not** shown in these sessions. A prototype session is a separate activity run only if stakeholder evidence shows what needs testing (`PROTOTYPE_VALIDATION_PLAN.md`).

## 6. Questions

### 6.1 Question bank by pathway step

Use the persona sets in `STAKEHOLDER_VALIDATION_PLAN.md` section 4 as the primary script. The twelve questions are:

1. What happens today after a potential NTD signal?
2. Where is it recorded?
3. Who assesses or prioritises it?
4. How is referral initiated?
5. Who owns referral completion?
6. What happens when the person does not arrive?
7. What information returns from the facility?
8. What tools do CHWs already use?
9. Would NTD-WATCH reduce or increase workload?
10. What programme decision would change?
11. Can existing DHIS2 or CHIS infrastructure support the workflow?
12. Where should a validated capability live?

### 6.2 Standard probes (neutral)

- "Can you tell me more about that?"
- "What happened next?"
- "Can you think of a recent example, without telling me who it was?"
- "How do you know that?" / "Who would know?"
- "Is that always the case, or does it vary?"
- "What does that look like on a busy day?"
- "Is there anything about this that works well already?"

### 6.3 Neutral description of the proposed steps (for Q9 and Q12 only)

> One idea being considered is a set of steps rather than a new standalone product. After someone notices a possible concern, a worker would answer some structured questions about the person's signs and circumstances. The system would suggest how urgently someone might be seen and what the usual next step is, as a prompt to a trained worker. It would not give a diagnosis or treatment. A referral would be noted, and a later record would show whether the person arrived and what happened, so that supervisors and programme staff can see which referrals are still open. We do not know whether this would be useful, whether it exists already, or whether it should live in a system you already use.

Then ask Q9 or Q12 as in the persona set. Do not describe benefits. Do not show screens.

### 6.4 Closing disconfirmation questions (all sessions)

- "What would make this unnecessary?"
- "What would make it harmful or unsafe?"
- "What would make you not use it?"
- "Is any of this already solved by something you use today?"
- "Who else should we speak to who might disagree with what you have said?"

## 8. No leading questions: test before use

A question may be used only if all answers below are "yes".

1. Would someone who liked the idea and someone who disliked it answer it the same way?
2. Does it avoid describing a benefit, a solution or the product's name?
3. Does it avoid a presumed problem ("Why is referral so poorly tracked?")? Rephrase as "How is referral tracked?"
4. Does it offer "none", "already solved", "don't know" and "would not use" as ordinary answers?
5. Is it one question, not two joined together?
6. Is it free of jargon the participant has not used?
7. Does it avoid asking the participant to approve, endorse or predict adoption?

| Leading                                           | Neutral                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "Wouldn't a tool that tracks referrals help you?" | "How are referrals tracked now, if at all?"                         |
| "How much time would this save you?"              | "Would the steps described take more, less or the same time? Why?"  |
| "Do you agree the current process is too slow?"   | "How long does it take from a report to action? Is that a problem?" |
| "Would you use NTD-WATCH?"                        | "What would make you use, or not use, something new for this?"      |

## 9. Bias controls

- **Problem before product.** No proposal until the current-practice sections are done.
- **Politeness bias.** Ask for examples; ask what they would not use; accept silence.
- **Power effects.** Separate sessions by seniority; no line managers present; no sponsor present.
- **Confirmation bias.** The note-taker records disconfirming statements with equal care. A devil's-advocate reviewer reads the notes before coding.
- **Facilitator independence.** Facilitators are not the developers; developers do not conduct sessions with dependants.
- **Anchoring.** Do not mention other participants' views, counts, or hypotheses.
- **Echoing.** Use the participant's words; do not paraphrase into project vocabulary during the session.
- **External evidence.** Do not tell participants what published sources say (for example about CIMS-NTDs, DHIS2 capabilities or the DHIS2 treatment-reporting platform) and then ask for agreement. Ask what they use and what happens; compare afterwards. Do not assume a global platform capability is configured or used in Nigeria; ask separately about what the platform can do, what is set up locally, and what workers actually do.
- **Selection.** Record who declined; include sceptics; avoid recruiting only through the sponsor.
- **Report negatives in full.** A strong finding against the idea is reported prominently.

## 10. Evidence classification

Every coded item carries one class (as in `STAGE6_VALIDATION_PLAN.md` section 7):

| Class | Applied when                                                                                   | Notes                                                                                                  |
| ----- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| SS    | A participant, in a recorded session, states a fact about their practice or setting            | Stakeholder evidence; role and date recorded; not generalised from one voice                           |
| XS    | An identified clinical, public-health or digital-health expert states a professional judgement | Expert evidence; conflicts declared                                                                    |
| OB    | Something observed in a prototype session                                                      | Not applicable in these interviews                                                                     |
| EF    | Something found in a document or system description, not said by the participant               | Never reclassified as SS; participants may confirm or contradict it, which is then a new SS or XS item |
| AS    | A belief or prediction (including a participant's guess about others or the future)            | Recorded as an assumption, not a fact                                                                  |
| UK    | The question was not answered or the participant did not know                                  | A gap, not evidence of absence                                                                         |

Confidence: **High** (two or more independent sources, or an owner of the system or process); **Medium** (one reliable source, or consistent secondary accounts); **Low** (single voice, hearsay, or prediction). A participant's prediction of what others do or will do is Low until corroborated.

## 11. Post-session coding approach

1. **Within 24 hours:** complete the note-taking record from notes (and recording, if consented). Remove identifying content.
2. **Code against the twelve questions:** for each question, record the participant's answer in a few words and the class (SS or XS), or UK.
3. **Code against hypotheses H1a, H1b, H1c, H2, H3, H4, H5:** mark each item as supporting, contradicting, mixed or not relevant to that hypothesis. Mark evidence about existing systems (CIMS-NTDs, WHO app, DHIS2, CHIS, WASHIMS) separately.
4. **Code against the locked gates G1 to G4** (`STAGE6_DECISION_REGISTER.md` section 3.2) as supporting, contradicting or unknown. A single item never satisfies a gate (section 3.5 there).
5. **Two coders** code each record independently; differences are discussed and recorded; the devil's-advocate reviewer checks that disconfirming evidence was not dropped.
6. **Enter coded findings** into `STAGE6_EVIDENCE_REGISTER.md` with status EVIDENCE FOUND; VALIDATED only when the sufficiency rules are met and the validation lead has reviewed.
7. **Update the assumption matrix (A1 to A15)** by persona: supported, contradicted, mixed, no evidence.
8. **Record contradictions and surprises**, including where stakeholder statements differ from external evidence (XE items).
9. **Do not compute or report percentages or scores** from small, non-random samples as if representative.
10. **Do not draft a recommendation** until the decision workshop has seen the coded evidence and the criteria are applied as locked.
